# Asset detail

Route: `/assets/[assetId]`
Who reaches it: anyone signed in, with no Role and no Job gate (#337) — an asset
off the reader's own jobs included (#509), which every list has shown since #593.
What
a reader DOES here — checking out, checking in, retiring and printing the label
— is a site manager's (#506), and since #597 only one assigned to the job the
item is on; everyone else reads the same screen, at both widths, without those
controls — a site manager on another job or on none among them.
Which width comes first: **phone**. Both widths must work; this one is
drawn first and the desktop is what it opens out into.

## What it answers

Which tool is this one, where is it, what has happened to it — and **can I take
it out or bring it back, right now?** Since #363 it also answers the last
question anybody asks about a tool: **this one is finished, take it off the
books.**

**That last one is why anybody opens this screen twice a day.** A project
starts with somebody scanning their job's tools out one at a time and ends
with them scanning back what returned, so the visit is: confirm the id
against the sticker, read the status, press once, and open the camera for
the next tool. Everything else on the page is read when that goes wrong.

**This is the only screen in the app a machine opens.** A QR code on a sticker
glued to the tool carries this address, and a phone camera is what will follow
it. Nothing else here is reached that way — every other detail screen is reached
from a list — and two things follow for a design.

**The reader is holding the object.** They are on a site, probably in gloves,
looking at a drill and at a phone. They already know which tool they scanned;
what they came for is where it is supposed to be and what has happened to it.

**The label does not carry this address, and that is what lets this one be
readable.** A QR encodes the whole URL, so the printed address is a short route
of its own, `/l/[labelCode]`, which redirects here and has a brief of its own.
This page took the name its collection gives it once that separation existed
(#348). The printed address is what may not move; this one may.

**A code is also printed in characters a person can read**, for a symbol that
has been scratched or painted over, so the second way here is somebody typing
it. Case does not matter when they do; the page then moves itself to the
canonical form of the address, so one asset keeps one address.

**It is drawn the design's way since #463**: 0n's record page at a desk (1f,
1g) and 1j's app screen on a phone. What follows says what each width carries
and which decisions in the drawing a later design may not undo.

## What it always carries

**identity.** The category's name as the heading — the record's name, as 0n and 1j
draw a record page — and the `Asset ID` beside it, set in the id face: under
the heading at a desk, leading the line under it, and over the name on a phone,
the first line of the screen under its top bar (#538), where the top bar held it
until then. Once the heading has scrolled under the top bar, the bar shows the
name. An asset whose category did not resolve is headed by its id. It was the
`Asset ID` as the heading, and nothing else, until #463.

**identity.** What the item is (1f, 1j, #519): its type and its category —
`HYE-AST-260909-004 · 26-USA-05 · Tool · Power tools` at a desk, the id first
and the item's job after it (#597), and `Tool · Power tools` under the name on
a phone, 4 under it, its values a step lighter there. It says what the name cannot: a name is the item's name and its size,
then a comma and its maker, then its part number in parentheses, `DEMO Angle
Grinder 4-1/2", DEMO Makita (GA4570)`, so it says neither which category nor
which type holds it. The type is information — `Equipment` or `Tool`, which
changes nothing this screen offers — and no control here reads it. A part the
category lacks is left out. From #507 to #519 the line under the heading was the
id and the job, and a second line under it said the class and the whole path,
`Class A · Tool > DEMO Machining > DEMO Angle Grinder > 4-1/2" > DEMO Makita >
GA4570`; from #519 the class followed the category, until #552 took it off the
catalog.

**The id is no longer the same string as the one on the sticker, and the
confirmation still works (#411).** The label prints `260909-004` where the page
reads `HYE-AST-260909-004`, so what a reader matches is the end of the id rather
than the whole of it — and at a desk the breadcrumb above the heading ends on
the printed code itself: `Equipment & Tools / DEMO Angle Grinder 4-1/2" /
260909-004`. **Wherever there is more than one job the path names the job the
asset is on between the list and the category** (1f, #519) — for every reader
since #593, where it was a reader on more than one of their own — a level that
opens every job by its code and takes them to the category's list on the one
they choose — `All jobs` stood first from #519 until #520 stood every list on
one job, and stands first again since #548, taking them to the category's list
on all of them; the level itself always names the asset's job, whatever list the
asset was opened from, since its address carries none and the asset is on one
job. `Equipment & Tools` and the category's own level carry the asset's job
(#581), whoever reads it since #593, so either list stands on the job the asset
is on. That is what the seven dropped characters were worth:
every asset carries them, so they confirmed nothing, and what is left on the
sticker is exactly the part that tells one asset from another. **A design
may not close the gap by shortening the id** — this screen names a record and
the base holds the long form — **and it may not close it by lengthening the
sticker**, which is the whole of #411.

**verdict.** Where the asset is: its status, one of `In stock`, `Out`,
`In repair` or `Retired`, behind the mark 0g gives it — a solid dot, a ring,
the ring dashed (0g's Away, #531), the ring struck through. This is what the
reader came for. **A value only one state has stands after it**: who holds a
tool that is out — the name its latest entry checked it out to — the day one in
repair was sent (#531), and the day a retired one was retired, each at both
widths; the retired day was a desk's alone until #538.

**evidence.** The item's two details (#533, #601): its **serial number**, the
maker's number printed on the item, and its **note**, the one line a site
manager keeps on it — what sets it apart from the others of its kind, a cracked
case, the crate it is kept in. At a desk they are the record rail's first block,
`Details`, a row each — `Serial` and `Note` in Ink 3, the value after them, the
serial number with even figures, a maker's number the portal's id face is not for
(1f; 0h, #607); on a phone, a line each 12 under the status, the serial number's
behind a barcode mark with even figures and the note's behind
a speech-bubble mark, in Ink 2, wrapping if it must (1j; 0a Value line, 0a Note).
Each is read by everybody wherever an item has one, on every status, a retired
one's and one in repair included; with neither, a reader who may write neither
meets no `Details` and no line. **Neither is an event**: writing one adds no
history entry and moves neither the status nor the job, so nothing on the screen
says who wrote it or when, and the category's list counts no change for it.
**Neither is on the label**, which prints the code alone. **A serial number is
one item's within its kind**: no other item of the same kind, retired ones
included and deleted ones not, may hold it, telling two apart by neither case
nor the spaces around it (#601).

**evidence.** The category it is one of, which is the heading, and the job it is
on, **named once, under the title (#597)**: after the id on the desk's line, and on
a phone first on the caption's line, before the type and the category (1j, the
files of 2026-11-04, where it stood on a line of its own behind a pin from #597)
— and wherever there are jobs to choose between, the breadcrumb
at a desk too, between the list and the category (below). The desk stated it under
the heading until #519, which the design dropped, and every history entry stated
it, with the phone's foot bar as the job the next event would be recorded on,
until #597 moved it back under the title and off every entry. **It is the job the
item sits on, and it says whose item it is to record**: only a site manager on it
records here. Both are single values, never lists — an asset is one unit of one
category and sits on one job.

**action.** For a site manager on the item's job (#506, #597), the one transition the status allows — `Check out` from `In stock`,
`Check in` from `Out`, `Return from repair` from `In repair` (#531) — recorded on
the item's own job, which it leaves the item on. There is never a choice of
transition: the status decides which one, and never a choice of job (#597). **The return asks nothing and records on the
press**: its row takes the item's own job, since it comes back to the job it
left, so a phone's foot bar is the press alone (1j-e) and a desk's header the
press beside `More actions` (1f). An item in repair cannot be checked out. **A desk asks it in
the header**, on the right and centered on the heading's line: the press or
the dialog it opens (1f, 1g). **A phone asks it in the foot bar** (1j): for a
check-out a field for who it goes to, and the press, at the foot of the screen
and riding on the keyboard — where the job stood first as a pill until #597.
**They are one transition in two drawings**: a phone on its side is wide enough
to draw the desk, and a dialog left open as it turns upright closes, the name it
was given standing in the foot bar (#495).

**A CHECK-OUT ALSO ASKS WHO THE TOOL IS GOING TO, AND A CHECK-IN DOES NOT.**
At a desk the check-out's dialog asks it in its one field, labeled
`Checked out to`, that reads `e.g. Jane Doe` while it is empty (#458) — the
job's field above it went with #597. On a
phone the foot bar's field reads `Checked out to` behind a person's mark, and
once it holds a name it ends in a ×, `Clear`, that empties it without opening
the sheet (1j-a). The
people it names have no account here, so it is free text rather than a picker
over a list of users. **A check-out cannot be recorded without one.** **The
dialog's `Check out` and the foot bar's press always act (#522), and a press
with no name is answered under the field**, `Enter a name.`, at both widths —
the desk dialog's words since #612, where both said 1k's `Enter a name to check
out.` — focus going to the field (1k-b). Nothing is written. Until #597 a press
with no job chosen was answered under the job the same way. A field of nothing but spaces is the
same refusal. Until #522 the dialog's `Check out` could not act until the field
held a name.

**Below the phone's edge, touching the name field opens a sheet**, titled with
the field's words, with the system keyboard up. A name can be typed there or
picked from the list below it, and both are the same value as the field behind.
It closes by picking, by its own `Done`, by the keyboard's done key, by
`Escape`, on its handle, dragged down and on what lies behind it, and focus
returns to the field. **A drag follows the finger and goes back when let go
short of closing, the keyboard staying up through it** (#580). **Opening it is
a press rather than a focus** — returning focus to the field would otherwise
reopen it. **At a desk there is no sheet**: the field is the one the
registration types a name in (1b), and the list opens under it. 1j's job sheet,
which the foot bar's pill opened for a person on several jobs, went with the
pill (#597).

**The list of names is headed `Recently at this job`** while nothing is typed,
and holds the people that job has recently handed tools to, most recent first.
**One entry per person however their name was typed** — `Mike R` and `mike r`
are one, shown in the most recent spelling — and **it is the item's job's list,
not the reader's** (#597), where it was the chosen job's and changed with the
choice. Typing narrows it, matching anywhere inside a name and ignoring case and
spacing, every name it matches, most recent first, and each name shows the part
that matched (#495). **A few are shown before anybody types and the number is a
display choice over the whole list**, so a design may set it: a name below the
cut is one keystroke away rather than absent.

**On a phone the list stands in a space of one height under the field, so the
sheet keeps its height while a name is typed (#589).** The space holds the
head and three rows; more scroll inside it, and the room fewer leave stays
empty. Its head is `Recently at this job` before anything is typed, `Matching
names` while what is typed matches, and `No past names match`, over no rows,
when it matches none. **On a job that has handed out nothing yet, the space is
empty** — no head and no rows, whatever is typed — because the list is about a
job's past names and there are none. It was empty before a job was chosen too,
until #597 left no job to choose.
**On a screen too short for the sheet and its keyboard the space gives way
first**, its rows scrolling under the head, and the field keeps its place. At a
desk the dialog's suggestions are as they were: the head goes once a fragment
is typed, and a list narrowed to nothing, or a job with no names, opens none.
Until #589 the phone's head and rows went once a typed name matched none, and a
job with none said `No items have gone out on this job yet.` in their place.

**The confirmation is that arriving does not act.** A scan opens the page, the
page states the id and the status, and one press is the transition. There is no
confirming dialog and there must not be one: this happens dozens of times a day,
and every confirmation in this app that IS a dialog is for something that cannot
be undone — a withdrawal, a deletion. A check-out is undone by the check-in the
same control offers a moment later. **The dialog a desk's check-out opens asks
and does not confirm** (#458): the name is what its row needs, and a check-in,
which needs none, records on the press — for a person on any number of jobs
since #597, where one on several was asked which in a dialog of the job alone.

**While the event is on its way the control says so (#469, #463):** the press,
the dialog's commitment or the foot bar's press keeps its color and its width
and, after a moment, draws a spinner in place of its words, saying
`Checking out…` or `Checking in…` to assistive tech — at both widths since
#495 — and nothing beside it takes a press. **The dialog's commitment never
waits for what is missing (#522, 0l)**: a press with the name missing sends
nothing, says so under the field, and moves focus to it, as the foot bar's press
does; until #522 it was drawn faded until the job and the name were given.

**action.** For a site manager on the item's job (#506, #597), `Retire this item` (#519; `Retire this tool` until then), behind `More actions` — at a desk a 32 icon
button beside the transition, named by its tooltip, and on a phone the 48
button at the top bar's right end (1f, 1j). It is in 0f's destructive red, and
opens a dialog. **From `In stock` alone, `Send for repair` stands above it
(#531; 1f, 1j-a)**, in Ink: it asks nothing and sends at once, as the return
that undoes it does, so its answer is said where a press's with no dialog is —
in a toast at a desk, under the status on a phone (#614) — and `More actions`
is busy while it is on its way. An item that is out is
checked in before it is sent; one in repair offers its return, and its menu the
retirement alone. **On a phone the menu ends on
the reader's account** — `Sign out` over their email, under a rule — since a
phone has no rail to name the reader in, and where the page offers no
retirement and none of the office's acts — a retired tool, or a reader who is
neither a site manager nor the office on an item nothing has happened to — it
holds the account alone (Tools 0a, #495, #583, #582). It is offered from
`In stock`, from `Out` and from `In repair` alike — a tool that broke on a
site is retired from there, one its maker gives up on is retired while it is
away, and requiring a check-in or a return first would put an event in the
history that did not happen.

**THE TWO CONTROLS MUST NOT READ AS THE SAME WEIGHT, AND THREE THINGS STOP
THEM.** One of them is pressed dozens of times a day and the other is that
asset's last. The first is where each is: the transition is the filled
button a desk's header and a phone's foot bar end on, and the retirement is an
item in a menu behind an icon, one press further away (#463). The second is
structural: the transition records on its press or opens the fields of an act
the next scan undoes; the retirement OPENS a question naming the tool, with a
red commitment (#458), so choosing it does nothing until a press inside the
dialog. The third is the wording: `Check out` names no object and `Retire this
tool` does. **A later design may move either and may not undo these** — in
particular it may not make the retirement a one-press submit, and it may not put
the two at a size and prominence that says they are the same kind of act.

**THE DESIGN PUT THE RETIREMENT IN A MENU, AND THIS IS WHAT THAT PLACEMENT IS
FOR.** A mis-press on it was already caught: it opens a dialog instead of acting,
and the dialog states what becomes true before anything happens. **So placement
is the second guard and not the only one**, and the menu adds no third — no
further confirmation, no disclosure inside the dialog. What the menu buys is the
mis-press made less likely in the first place: a phone's foot bar holds only the
act a scan is for, and the top bar's button is where a person goes looking for
the rarer one.

**What the dialog carries**, in this order: a heading that asks with the category's
name — `Retire DEMO Rotary Hammer?` for that one — and the asset's id under
it (#458), an account of what becomes true, and the two ways out, `Retire item`
and `Cancel` — the confirm names what it retires, as the opener does (#455). The
account is the design's since #458, `It will be removed from inventory, and no
more check-outs or check-ins can be recorded. This can't be undone.`: the asset
stops counting as something the company holds, nothing more can be
recorded against it, and nothing undoes it. That its row and its whole history
stay, the third fact #363 wrote, is what the page goes on showing once the
status is the end. **That account is the point of the dialog rather than
decoration**: it is the same voice the three deletion confirmations use, and
the shared brief calls it copy doing work a visual cannot.

**It takes no input of any kind — no reason, and no job.** A design must not
add a field to it. It is a sentence and two ways out, and that is the whole of
it.

**It asks for no reason, and that is a decision rather than a gap.** A required
reason on this event was recorded as a rule for a long time and was dropped:
one of its two grounds went when `Lost` left the status vocabulary, and the
other — that the act cannot be undone — is what the dialog itself now bears.
A design must not add a free-text field back; there is no field behind it.

**THE JOB IS NOT ASKED FOR HERE, NOR ANYWHERE ON THIS SCREEN SINCE #597.**
Retiring moves nothing, so the row records where the asset already was, as every
event here does now: the item's job, under its title. **A design must not put a
job control on this screen** — the transition had one until #597, because a scan
was taken for the person handling the tool, and a check-in could move the item to
the reader's job; the first version of this dialog had a second one, and one
screen asking the same question twice let the two answers disagree. The dialog
does not restate the job, and it carries no line about moving the asset — a
retirement is not a move.

**If the item moves, or the reader is taken off its job, while the page is
open, the press is refused and said (#597).** The office may change an item's
job (#583), and the office assigns jobs; either can happen between drawing the
page and pressing. Every press carries the job the page was drawn on, and the
action, reading the item and the reader again, refuses it on the page drawn
again, by what changed (#614). An item whose job is not the one the page was
drawn on says `This item moved to 26-USA-07. Nothing was saved.`, naming the job
it is on now, whether the reader records there or not — the press is refused
rather than recorded somewhere they did not see. One that did not move, whose
reader no longer records on it — taken off its job, or no longer a site manager
— says `You no longer have permission to update this item. Nothing was
saved.` Either says the same whatever was pressed, the retirement's and the two
details' included, and each is said where its press was (below).

**The transition has no heading.** The two blocks below it name things —
`History`, `Label` — and a heading over the act would have to name it in the
abstract, which is a word the app does not say. The control names itself.

**THE TOUCH TARGETS ARE THE DESIGN'S, AND THEY ARE THE FIRST IN THE APP THAT ARE
FUNCTIONAL RATHER THAN AESTHETIC.** Every other control in this app is clicked
with a mouse at a desk. These are pressed on a phone, on a site, by somebody who
may be wearing gloves, and the design sets them (Tools 0a): the foot bar's press
50 tall and the screen's width, every target at least 48 square, and the menu's
row 48. **A target too
small to hit in gloves is not an inelegance** — it is a transition somebody
writes on paper instead, and the app then holds nothing — so a later design may
not shrink these below the phone's Target.

**evidence.** At a desk, the label this asset's sticker carries, in the
record rail beside the history: the label's own page as the labels' dialog
draws it, at twice its size on the Field ground, with **the print size in
text** under it — `Prints at 11 × 12 mm on 12 mm tape`, one line in Ink 3 (1f,
the files of 2026-11-04, where `Size` and `Symbol` stood as two figures from
#463) — and, for a site manager, a control that prints it (#506). **A phone
draws no label**: a label is printed at a desk, and the phone's screen is for
the scan. **The label, its size and its symbol are for every reader**: what a
asset's sticker carries is a fact about it, and only printing one is a site
manager's.

**Why it is here decides how it is drawn.** It is here so a reader can SEE what a
replacement sticker will carry and match it against the one in their hand — not to
be scanned off the screen. Somebody who typed the id because the sticker had worn
through is already on this page, so a scan of the screen would return them where they
are. **So it has no size of its own**: it is drawn at whatever reads in its place
(0p), which is the dialog's preview scale, and the printed size is a fact the two
figures state rather than a size the drawing keeps. Until #463 the symbol alone was
drawn at its printed size with a line saying so.

**It is named for assistive tech**, `QR label for this item`, as an image: the
code under the symbol is nothing the heading says, so it takes a name rather than
hiding. The string sat in the page's copy with nothing drawing it from #352 to
#463.

**action.** For a site manager on the item's job (#506, #597), a control that prints this asset's label, `Print label`. It opens
the labels' dialog on this one label (#457), rather than printing the page — that
dialog owns the label's page and the print rule, so printing the page would be a
second layout of one label.

**It does not say `reprint`, and the reason is that the screen cannot know.**
Nothing in this base records whether a sticker was ever printed or stuck on, so a
control promising a re-print would state something the app cannot check — and the
two readers are after the same act anyway: one whose label has worn through, and
one printing a label for the first time.

**evidence.** A history, one entry per `Asset Log` row, **the newest first** (1f,
1j) — the latest scan is the one a reader standing over the tool came to check.
It read oldest first until #463. Each entry carries three facts and always all
three — the event, when it happened, and who recorded it. **There is no fourth
and nothing is ever absent** but on a check-out, below. **No entry names its job
since #597** (1f, 1j): the files say a log line never repeats the job, which the
title block names once, and every event after a registration records on the job
the item sits on. The base keeps every row's job, which is the history of where
the item was.

**Each width lays an entry out its own way, and neither labels a fact.** At a desk
the event heads the entry and one line under it says when and `by` whom. On a
phone the event stands alone at 16, who a check-out went to under it behind a
person's mark, and the moment and who recorded it last. Who
recorded it is named in full, first and last name, as a place naming who did
something is (#463).

**The event is printed as the base stores it: `Created`, `Checked out`,
`Checked in`, `Sent for repair`, `Returned from repair` or `Retired`.**
The two repair events are #531's, the past of their controls' own words. Each is the design's word: `Created` was
`Registered` until #455, and the two scans were `Checked Out` and `Checked In`
until #463 took the design's sentence case. The base renamed each value in
place, so every entry says the new word and none says an old one. **The case
is this axis's own** — the materials screens still print theirs in title case,
`In Review`, `Sent to Vendor` — because a status's screen word and its base
value are one string, and an axis takes the design's words when the design
settles them (`naming.md`).

**The instant renders as a date and a time to the minute, in the design's
notation** — `09/14/2026` with its slashes dimmed and the time after it with no
comma, as every screen in the app writes one since #463. What an entry carries
is a moment somebody reads, and seconds are finer than the resolution a tool
moves at.

**It is the reader's own zone, and that is why no zone is named (#374).** The
moment resolves against the phone in the reader's hand rather than against the
server, so there is nothing to convert and nothing to label — the same reason a
phone's own clock carries no zone. **A design may assume the reader's zone here.**

**It appears a moment after the rest of the entry**, because only the browser can
say what zone the reader is in and it cannot say so until the page is
interactive. Until then the entry's other facts are there and this one is
blank. A design may not reserve a placeholder that reads as a value, and it may
not assume the facts arrive together.

## What it carries only sometimes

**A refusal of the press, said where the press was (#614).** At a desk, in the
dialog or question that sent it, over its actions: it stands until the reader
closes it, showing what it was opened on, and its commitment is turned away and
names the sentence when the page drawn again no longer offers the act. A desk's
press with no dialog — a check-in, a return from repair, `Send for repair` —
says it in a toast at the foot of the page, which stays until it is dismissed or
the next press. On a phone, the notice under the status in the title block
(1k), the sheet or question that sent it closed. Those that reach it: someone else has
already recorded something, so the press was not saved; the item is no longer
the reader's to record, or moved to another of their jobs, since the page was
drawn (#597, above), the retirement's question and the two details' included;
the reader is no longer a site manager (#614); the asset carries no such id; and
the event was recorded but the asset's own status was not updated. A line
saying a check-in would move the item to another job stood under the dialog's
job until #597, when no press moved an item any more. The last is the only
one that means something was written, and its sentence says so — `This wasn't
fully saved. Try again to finish.` since #614, and `This wasn't fully recorded.
Do it again to finish.` from #612, where until then it named the event, the item
and the status the lists still read — and pressing again fixes it. A loan left half done says the same in its own act: `The item wasn't fully
lent to 26-USA-12. Lend it again to finish.` and `The item wasn't fully
returned. Return it again to finish.` Ordinarily nothing is said.

**EVERY REFUSAL ARRIVES ON A FRESHLY RENDERED PAGE, AND THAT IS WHAT THE FIRST
SENTENCE IS WRITTEN AGAINST.** The status, the control's direction and the
history are all re-read and re-drawn in the same moment the sentence appears,
so the screen under a refusal is never the screen the reader pressed on. It
also means a refused press and a successful one leave the SAME screen — a
flipped control, a moved status, one more history entry — and the only visible
difference is whose entry it is. So the sentence names the person and the
moment, the design's (1k) since #463: `Jisoo Park already checked this out on
10/05/2026 8:50 AM. Your check-out wasn't saved.` — who recorded the latest
entry, in full, and that entry's own moment in the reader's zone, written as
every date on the screen is. It says `already` since #495, as the design's
final files do. **A design may not treat it as an aside.** It is the one thing
distinguishing two outcomes that otherwise look alike, it is read on a phone in
one hand, and it names no status because the status is stated directly above it
and was just refreshed.

**The retirement's dialog, which is closed until `Retire this item` is chosen.**
The page behind it stays legible, and it closes by `Cancel`, by `Escape`, by its
close at a desk and on a press behind it as a phone's sheet, which draws no
handle and is not dragged (#580), with focus returning
to `More actions`; the confirm takes it away with the page's offer, and focus
goes to the page's heading (#458). It does not close while a confirmation is in

## What must agree elsewhere

**The facts on a history entry are invariants of the base, not choices.**
The event is one of a closed set of four; the job is on every row and is never
blank; who recorded it is written on every path that appends a row; and who a
tool went to is on every `Checked out` row and on no other. A design that
hides one is hiding a defect upstream rather than simplifying a row.

**A repeated job down the history is the normal reading.** An asset that has
never left its job carries the same job on every entry. That the column has no
blanks is what makes the previous entry's job readable as the previous job,
which is why nothing stores a former job — collapsing the repetition would take
that away.

**The status shown here is the same value the category list counts.** It is
maintained by the app rather than computed on this screen, so this page and
the two list screens cannot disagree about where an asset is.

**And it is the value both of this screen's controls move.** A transition here
changes what `/asset-categories` counts and what the category's own screen shows in its
`Status` column — the first non-zero `Out` either of them has ever rendered
came from the transition control, and the first non-zero `Retired` from the
other. The verdict and the actions are about one fact, which is why they sit
with the status rather than with the history.

**This screen's dialogs close on `Escape` and hand focus back, and most of the
app's do not.** Every dialog on this axis does since #458, in the design's frame;
the rest close only by their own controls. That is an inconsistency the app has
rather than one this screen introduces, and the shared brief records it beside the
modal-styling constraint — a design pass over modals should settle it for all
of them rather than for this one.

**A history entry appears the moment a transition is confirmed, and that
arrival is the whole confirmation.** No banner, no toast, no line under the
heading, and nothing in the address: the status has flipped, an entry heads the
history, and the control now reads the other way. **A design that adds a success
message here is undoing a decision**, the same one the shared brief records for
every other create and edit in the app.

**The job on a history entry is where the asset was, and that is not always
the actor's.** A scan takes it from whoever pressed the control, because they
are holding the tool. A retirement takes it from the asset, because
retiring moves nothing. Either way it is stored at that moment and never looked
up again — so **a check-in on a different job from the check-out before it is a
true record of a tool changing site**, and a retirement always matching the row
before it is what keeps that reading available.

**The two tables are not interchangeable, and the screen names them the
design's way (#455, #519).** An `Asset Categories` row is a category — the kind, a row of the
catalog the office keeps (#507), whose name heads this page. An `Assets` row — this object, with this
printed id, which this brief calls an asset — is an `item` in every sentence
about it since #519: `Retire this item`, `Item not found`, `This item was last scanned
on …`. A row of the category list is an `item` as well, and no sentence sets the two side
by side; from #455 to #519 both were a `tool` and the assets were counted as items on
their category's own page. **No sentence on the screen says `tool item` since #463**,
which took the design's `Tool not found` for the last one that did.

**The label in the rail and the label the dialog prints are one object, built
once for both (#457), and drawn alike (#463)**: the rail draws the dialog's own
page at the dialog's preview scale, 19.14 mm for a symbol that prints at 9.57.
Both read one module size, the floor a print proved (#467), and multiply it by
the side count that symbol actually came out at, so a longer address makes a
bigger symbol on both rather than a denser one on either — and the `Symbol`
figure followed it, until the files of 2026-11-04 left the rail saying the size
alone. A design that pins the drawing to a box in pixels breaks the
equality with the printed label, and nothing on screen would show it.

**This screen prints nothing itself.** Its print control opens the labels'
dialog on the label this page built (#457), and it was a link to the label
screen until then. That is what keeps a replacement the same physical object as
the original by construction — there is no second layout to drift from the
dialog's.

**There are two ways in, and both survive a reload.** A scan of the label is the
first, arriving through the short route that redirects here; the second is a
category's own screen, which lists every asset under it with a link to this one
(#339) and is reached from the category list and from a registration, which lands
there with what it wrote selected (#449). **This said three ways, one of them
lost on a reload**: the registration form's own answer linked here too until
#449, and that list was gone once the page was.
